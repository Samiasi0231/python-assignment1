# Mountain Peak Outfitters Database Analysis Findings

## Overview

This report summarizes the findings from the analysis of Mountain Peak Outfitters' inventory, sales, customers, employees, and product performance. The analysis is based on the sample PostgreSQL dataset provided for the project. Several records in the dataset intentionally contain missing relationships or orphaned data, which were considered when developing the SQL queries and interpreting the results.

---

# Inventory Management

## Question 1: Which products have less than 20 items in stock?

Six products have fewer than 20 units available in inventory: Trailblazer Hiking Boot, Orphan Product, Everest Tent 4-Person, NavPro GPS Device, Campfire Portable Stove, and Pathfinder 55L Backpack. The Trailblazer Hiking Boot has no available inventory, while several other products have stock levels that may require monitoring.

The company should prioritize replenishing the out-of-stock product and evaluate the demand patterns for the remaining low-stock products. Products such as the NavPro GPS Device and Everest Tent may represent potential lost-sales risks if demand continues while inventory remains limited.

---

## Question 2: What products are currently out of stock?

The analysis identified the Trailblazer Hiking Boot as the only product with a stock quantity of zero. This means the product is currently unavailable for purchase and may result in missed sales opportunities if customer demand exists.

Management should investigate whether the product is awaiting replenishment, has been discontinued, or requires a new supplier order. Monitoring stock levels regularly would help ensure that popular products do not remain unavailable for extended periods.

---

## Question 3: Which products have the highest profit margins?

The highest profit margins are generated by the water bottle products, particularly Adventure Water Bottle and EcoTrek Water Bottle, both with profit margins of approximately 80%. TrailSupport Hiking Poles and Orphan Product also have relatively high profit margins compared with many of the larger outdoor products.

These findings indicate that lower-priced accessories can provide significant profitability despite having lower individual selling prices. Management may benefit from promoting high-margin accessories through product bundles, cross-selling, or point-of-sale recommendations.

---

## Question 4: Which products have no assigned category or supplier?

The analysis identified one product, Orphan Product, that does not have an assigned category or supplier. Both its `category_id` and `supplier_id` are NULL, meaning it is not fully connected to the rest of the inventory management structure.

This represents a data-quality issue that could affect reporting, supplier management, and category-based analysis. Management should assign the product to an appropriate category and supplier or confirm whether the product should remain active in the database.

---

## Question 5: List products with their category and supplier information

The product analysis successfully links most products with their corresponding categories and suppliers. The Orphan Product is the exception, as it does not have a category or supplier assigned and must be preserved through the use of LEFT JOINs.

Maintaining complete category and supplier relationships is important for inventory planning and purchasing decisions. The presence of orphaned records highlights the importance of validating foreign-key relationships and ensuring product information is complete.

---

# Sales Analysis

## Question 6: What is the total sales amount for each store?

Mountain Peak Denver generated the highest total sales, followed by Mountain Peak Seattle and Mountain Peak Boston. Several stores, including Austin, Nashville, Minneapolis, and Empty Store, recorded no sales in the provided dataset.

Denver appears to be the strongest performing store based on total revenue. Management should investigate whether the zero-sales stores are newly opened, inactive, or simply not represented in the available sales period before making performance decisions.

---

## Question 7: What are the total sales and transaction counts for each month of 2023?

Sales activity varied across the first six months of 2023. May generated the highest total sales amount and also had the largest number of transactions, while June had the lowest activity because only one transaction was recorded.

The increase in May may indicate stronger customer demand, seasonal purchasing behavior, or successful sales activity during that period. Additional data from future months and previous years would help determine whether this pattern is seasonal or temporary.

---

## Question 8: Which product categories generate the most revenue?

Tents generated the highest revenue among all product categories, followed by Jackets and GPS Devices. The high revenue generated by tents is influenced by their relatively high selling prices and consistent sales activity.

Management should consider tents a strategically important category because of their strong contribution to revenue. Inventory availability, supplier reliability, and promotional campaigns for this category should be monitored closely to avoid lost sales opportunities.

---

## Question 9: What are the top five most frequently purchased products?

EcoTrek Water Bottle was the most frequently purchased product, with a substantially higher quantity sold than most other products. Expedition 3-Person Tent and several other products followed behind it.

The strong demand for EcoTrek Water Bottle, combined with its high profit margin, makes it a particularly valuable product for the business. Management may consider maintaining higher safety stock levels and promoting similar accessory products to increase sales.

---

## Question 10: What are the purchasing patterns of each customer?

The analysis shows the number of purchases and most recent purchase date for every customer. The Non-purchasing Customer has no recorded transactions and therefore has no most recent purchase date.

Including customers with no purchases is important because they may represent opportunities for marketing and customer re-engagement. The business could use targeted promotions or loyalty incentives to encourage these customers to make their first purchase.

---

# Customer Insights

## Question 11: Which customers have spent the most?

Alex Roberts recorded the highest total customer spending, followed by Ava King and Sophia Walker. These customers represent some of the most financially valuable customers in the available dataset.

High-spending customers should be considered for loyalty benefits, personalized promotions, and retention strategies. Understanding what products and categories these customers purchase could also help the company create targeted marketing campaigns.

---

## Question 12: Does loyalty tier correlate with product ratings?

Silver-tier customers provided the highest average ratings, while Bronze-tier customers provided the lowest average ratings. Gold and None-tier customers fell between these groups.

The available data suggests that customer satisfaction may vary across loyalty tiers, but the dataset is too small to establish a strong statistical correlation. Management should collect more reviews and customer feedback before drawing firm conclusions about the relationship between loyalty status and satisfaction.

---

## Question 13: Which customers have purchased products but never left a review?

The analysis did not identify any customers who made purchases without leaving a product review. Every customer with a recorded purchase also appears in the product review data.

Although this pattern is unusual in a real-world business environment, it demonstrates that the dataset has strong review participation among purchasing customers. In a larger dataset, identifying customers who do not leave reviews could help management develop review-request campaigns.

---

## Question 14: Which customers increased their spending in Q2 compared with Q1?

Four customers increased their spending during the second quarter of 2023 compared with the first quarter: Emma Young, Jacob Allen, Matthew Wright, and Charlotte Scott. Charlotte Scott showed a particularly notable increase because she had no purchases during Q1 but made purchases during Q2.

These customers demonstrate increasing engagement with the business and may represent opportunities for retention and targeted marketing. Management could examine which products drove their increased spending and use that information to create similar recommendations for other customers.

---

## Question 15: What are the favorite product categories for Gold-tier customers?

Gold-tier customers did not have a single favorite category. Hiking Boots, Jackets, Tents, and Water Bottles were tied as the most frequently purchased categories among these customers.

This suggests that Gold-tier customers have diverse purchasing interests rather than concentrating on one specific type of product. Marketing campaigns targeted at Gold customers should therefore consider cross-category recommendations and product bundles.

---

# Employee Performance

## Question 16: Which employees are the top-performing sales associates?

Robert Taylor generated the highest total sales among the sales associates, followed by Jessica Davis and Laura Jackson. These employees contributed strongly to the company's sales performance during the available period.

Management should examine the sales practices and customer engagement strategies of top-performing employees. Their methods may provide useful insights that can be shared through training or mentoring programs.

---

## Question 17: Which employees generate the highest average transaction value?

William White generated the highest average transaction value in the dataset. However, this result is based on a limited number of transactions, so it should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of consistently superior performance.

Robert Taylor also demonstrated a strong average transaction value while handling more than one transaction. When evaluating employee performance, management should consider both transaction value and transaction volume rather than relying on averages alone.

---

## Question 18: What are the employee and sales characteristics of each store?

The analysis combined store information with manager assignments, employee counts, and total sales. Denver had the strongest sales performance, while several stores had no employees or sales recorded in the dataset.

One notable finding is that Mountain Peak Portland recorded a sale despite having no assigned employees. This occurred because one sale record does not have an associated employee. This highlights a data-quality issue that should be addressed to ensure accurate employee performance reporting.

---

## Question 19: Which stores have above-average employee salaries?

Seattle, Boston, Miami, and Chicago had average employee salaries higher than the company-wide employee salary average. Miami recorded the highest average salary among the stores included in the analysis.

Higher salary levels may reflect differences in management structure, employee experience, regional labor markets, or staffing composition. Management should compare salary levels with sales performance and productivity to determine whether compensation levels align with business results.

---

## Question 20: How do products perform based on sales volume and profit margin?

The product performance matrix classified products into Stars, Volume Drivers, Opportunities, and Problems using average sales volume and average profit margin as the classification thresholds. Products such as EcoTrek Water Bottle performed strongly because they combined relatively high sales volume with high profit margins.

The matrix can help management prioritize inventory and marketing decisions. Stars should be protected through reliable inventory availability, Volume Drivers should be evaluated for margin improvement, Opportunities may benefit from additional promotion, and Problems may require pricing, marketing, or product strategy review.

---

# Advanced Analysis

## Question 21: What does the employee management hierarchy look like?

The management hierarchy shows that each major store with employees has a Store Manager at the top, followed by Assistant Managers and Sales Associates. The hierarchy was generated using the self-referencing `manager_id` relationship in the employees table.

The analysis also revealed an employee who is not assigned to a store or manager. While this record is useful for testing database queries, similar situations in a production database should be reviewed to ensure that organizational reporting structures remain accurate.

---

## Question 22: How do purchasing patterns differ across loyalty tiers?

The comprehensive customer analysis shows differences in customer spending, transaction behavior, preferred product categories, and product review activity across the loyalty tiers. Gold-tier customers showed broad purchasing behavior across multiple categories, while other tiers displayed stronger concentration in particular product categories.

These findings can support more targeted marketing strategies. Rather than treating all customers the same, Mountain Peak Outfitters could use loyalty-tier purchasing patterns to create personalized promotions, category recommendations, and retention campaigns.

---

# Overall Conclusion

The analysis shows that Mountain Peak Outfitters has several strong revenue and profitability opportunities. Tents generate the highest category revenue, while EcoTrek Water Bottle stands out as a particularly strong product because it combines high sales volume with a strong profit margin. Denver is the highest-performing store, and Robert Taylor is the strongest sales associate based on total sales.

The analysis also identified several operational and data-quality issues, including low inventory levels, an out-of-stock product, products without category or supplier assignments, sales without an associated employee, and stores without recorded activity. Addressing these issues would improve the reliability of management reporting and support better inventory, staffing, and marketing decisions.
